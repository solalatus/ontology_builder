# Majority-vote merge of three interviews vs. the LLM cascade (issue #174, phase 1)

Generated by `tests/evals/majority-vote-merge.mjs` from `results.json`. Design pre-registered in
[`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md), committed before any result. Offline: committed interviews only, no API calls.

**Verdict: not convincing** by the pre-registered criterion (majority beats the
three-way cascade in both eras, and is level with the two-way merge in at least one).

- Beats the three-way cascade in both eras: **no**.
- Level with the two-way merge (within 0.005) in at least one era: **no**.

## Why: what the vote throws away

Elements the vote drops (in the union of three, not in the majority), pooled over the five domains,
and how many of them matched gold. Approximate: gold matching is one-to-one, so a dropped element's
match can move to another element.

| Era | Classes dropped (correct) | Relationships dropped (correct) | Properties dropped (correct) |
|---|---|---|---|
| current | 36 (33, 92%) | 144 (41, 28%) | 91 (47, 52%) |
| pre-152 | 31 (24, 77%) | 75 (31, 41%) | 84 (51, 61%) |

Interviews are recall-limited: a class only one interview found is usually real, so voting discards
correct classes and the composite falls. Relationships are the opposite: most single-interview
relationships are wrong, and voting raises their precision. The vote helps where noise is the
problem and hurts where incompleteness is.

**Not tested here, a hypothesis only:** a hybrid that takes the union of classes and votes on
relationships. It was suggested by these results, so testing it on the same runs would be circular;
it needs its own pre-registration.

- **union(1,2) vs. merge(1,2)** is the pre-registered control for "deterministic vs. LLM
  reconciliation" on the same two interviews. On this heuristic score the LLM merge adds nothing measurable (under 0.01 in every era) over a plain union.
  The scorer matches wording, and the union keeps the interviews' wording while the LLM merge rephrases,
  so this needs the semantic judge (phase 2) before it is believed.
- **union vs. merge(1,2,3):** a plain union of all three beats the LLM three-way cascade in every era, so the cascade's third step loses more than the noise of a three-way union explains.
- **Best pair chosen without gold** (fewest consistency errors and warnings), against a random pick
  among the three pair unions (their average): current −0.003, pre-152 −0.041. The deterministic checker does not pick better pairs than chance here. Choices per domain:
  current: brick-hvac (1,3), fibo-loans (1,2), iof-maintenance (2,3), iof-supply-chain (1,2), itops (1,2).
  pre-152: brick-hvac (1,2), fibo-loans (2,3), iof-maintenance (2,3), iof-supply-chain (2,3), itops (1,3).

## Exploratory, not pre-registered: how much of "merging two helps" is the choice of pair

The cascade only ever merged runs 1 and 2. Run numbering is arbitrary, so the deterministic unions of
all three pairs (scored for the best-pair baseline above) show what merging two interviews gives on
average.

| Era | union(1,2) | union(1,3) | union(2,3) | average pair | union of all 3 | single-run mean | merge(1,2), LLM |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| current | 0.742 | 0.669 | 0.665 | 0.692 | 0.691 | 0.654 | 0.741 |
| pre-152 | 0.725 | 0.732 | 0.686 | 0.714 | 0.738 | 0.642 | 0.723 |

- current: merging two interviews gains +0.038 over a single interview on average (+0.088 for the pair the cascade used); the union of all three gains +0.037.
- pre-152: merging two interviews gains +0.072 over a single interview on average (+0.081 for the pair the cascade used); the union of all three gains +0.095.

Reading: every pair union and the union of all three beat a single interview on average, but the published two-way figure depends on which pair was labelled (1,2). The union of all three is about as good as an average pair or better (within 0.005 or above, in every era), and needs no choice of pair.
The LLM merge was only run on pair (1,2), so the other pairs use the deterministic union, which matched
the LLM merge on that pair.
