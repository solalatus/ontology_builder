Findings

Addressing the three specific questions this replication set was designed to answer, honestly — including where the anchor run turned out not to be representative.

Repeats — confirmed in all 3 runs

The concept–structure gap

Classes are recovered at roughly 2–10× the rate of relationships in every run, both heuristic and semantic, both denominators. This is the single most stable pattern across the replication set — more stable than any individual metric's own value.

Full-domain class vs. relationship recall (heuristic): 30.9→9.3, 30.9→8.3, 26.5→2.8
Substantial, metric-dependent

Run-to-run variance

The equal-weighted composite is tight (full-domain heuristic: 37.0–39.6%, a 2.6-point range) because sub-metric swings partly offset each other. Individual sub-metrics vary far more: full-domain relationship recall swings more than 3× (2.8%–9.3%), and controlled-value fidelity swings 33.5 points (62.4%–95.9%) — plausibly a small-sample artifact, since only a handful of controlled-value properties get matched in any single run and a couple of wording differences move the average sharply.

Composite range: 2.2 pt (full-domain heuristic) vs. 33.5 pt (value fidelity, same table)
Repeated, not a one-off — the anchor undersold it further

Low property recall

Full-domain property recall is low in 2 of 3 runs (run-01: 11.7%, run-03: 12.6%) and markedly higher only in run-02 (26.1%). The anchor run's own number is representative of the majority pattern, not an unlucky outlier — if anything, run-02 is the outlier, and reading only the anchor would have understated how variable this metric is, not overstated the problem.

Full-domain property recall: 11.7%, 26.1%, 12.6% — median 12.6%, matches run-01 and run-03

Coverage also flatters this dimension. Now that properties are matched one-to-one and carry a precision, run-02's stronger recall (26.1% full-domain, 50.0% scoped) resolves to an F1 of 28.4% and 21.8%: those models list 41–93 properties each, and most match nothing in the reference at all.

Observed, not confirmed as causal

Run-03's behavioral outlier: far fewer state-checks

Run-03 called get_graph_state only 5 times against 43–46 times in runs 1–2, despite having the most turns (57) and the most applied edits (48). This coincides with run-03 also being the run with the lowest relationship recall by a wide margin. Presented as a correlation worth investigating in future work, not asserted as the cause — a 3-run set can't establish that on its own.

get_graph_state calls: 46 (run-01), 43 (run-02), 5 (run-03) · relationship recall (full, heuristic): 9.3%, 8.3%, 2.8%

What this does and doesn't resolve

  • Removes: the "single stochastic run" objection — the reported anchor numbers are now shown alongside two independent replications under the identical frozen configuration, not presented as if they were the only possible outcome.
  • Does not resolve: the development–evaluation overlap (the prompt was iteratively tuned against this same fixture before this replication set existed).
  • Does not resolve: the absence of unseen-domain transfer evidence — all three runs use the same fixture.
  • Does not resolve: the absence of real human–agent interview data — the persona side remains a single-pass simulated LLM.
  • No further tuning: runs 2 and 3 are replications under a configuration frozen after run-01 — nothing about the prompt, fixture, models, or scoring code was changed in response to their results.
